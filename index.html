Foundational Fluency Component

  • Simplify complex (nested) fractions

1. Worked Examples

These three examples cover the three core moves for this page: simplifying by factoring and canceling, clearing denominators to solve an equation, and simplifying a fraction that contains fractions inside it. Examples 2 and 3 each show two valid solution methods side by side — try each step yourself before revealing it.

The one habit that makes algebraic fractions manageable

You can only cancel factors, never individual terms. \( \dfrac{PK}{QK} = \dfrac{P}{Q} \) is valid because \( K \) is a factor of both the top and bottom — but nothing in \( \dfrac{x+3}{x} \) can be canceled, since \( x \) is only a term of the numerator, not a factor of the whole thing.

Multiplication: \( \dfrac{P}{Q}\cdot\dfrac{R}{S} = \dfrac{PR}{QS} \)    Division: \( \dfrac{P}{Q}\div\dfrac{R}{S} = \dfrac{P}{Q}\cdot\dfrac{S}{R} \)    Same-denominator addition: \( \dfrac{P}{Q}+\dfrac{R}{Q} = \dfrac{P+R}{Q} \)

Example 1 — Simplify by factoring first

Try this yourself before revealing the steps.

Simplify: \( \dfrac{x^2-16}{x^2+x-12} \)

Step 1: Factor the numerator — it's a difference of squares. \[ x^2-16 = (x-4)(x+4) \]
Step 2: Factor the denominator — find two numbers that multiply to \(-12\) and add to \(1\). \[ x^2+x-12 = (x+4)(x-3) \]
Step 3: Now that both are factored, cancel the common factor \( (x+4) \). \[ \dfrac{(x-4)(x+4)}{(x+4)(x-3)} = \dfrac{x-4}{x-3}, \quad x \neq -4 \]
Answer: \( \dfrac{x-4}{x-3} \), with \( x \neq -4 \) (and \( x \neq 3 \) so the original denominator isn't zero)

Example 2 — Two valid ways to solve a rational equation

Try this yourself before revealing the steps. Both methods below reach the same answer — compare them and decide which one feels more natural to you.

Solve: \( \dfrac{x}{4} + \dfrac{3}{x} = 2 \)

Method 1 — Combine into a single fraction, then clear it

Step 1: Combine the left side into one fraction using the common denominator \( 4x \). \[ \dfrac{x}{4} + \dfrac{3}{x} = \dfrac{x^2}{4x} + \dfrac{12}{4x} = \dfrac{x^2+12}{4x} \]
Step 2: Now you have one fraction equal to a number — clear it by multiplying both sides by \( 4x \) (equivalent to cross-multiplying). \[ \dfrac{x^2+12}{4x} = 2 \implies x^2+12 = 8x \]
Step 3: Move everything to one side and solve like any quadratic. \[ x^2-8x+12=0 \implies (x-6)(x-2)=0 \implies x=6 \text{ or } x=2 \]

Method 2 — Fraction Busters: clear every denominator immediately

Step 1: Multiply every term on both sides by the common denominator, \( 4x \), all at once — this is what clears the fractions. \[ 4x\cdot\dfrac{x}{4} + 4x\cdot\dfrac{3}{x} = 4x\cdot 2 \]
Step 2: Simplify each term — the fractions cancel out entirely. \[ x^2 + 12 = 8x \]
Step 3: Same equation as Method 1 from here — factor and solve. \[ x^2-8x+12=0 \implies (x-6)(x-2)=0 \implies x=6 \text{ or } x=2 \]
Answer (either method): \( x=6 \) or \( x=2 \) — both check out in the original equation, since neither makes a denominator zero

Example 3 — Two valid ways to simplify a complex fraction

Try this yourself before revealing the steps. Both methods below reach the same answer — compare them and decide which one feels more natural to you.

Simplify: \( \dfrac{1+\dfrac{2}{x}}{1-\dfrac{4}{x^2}} \)

Method 1 — Combine into single fractions, then divide

Step 1: Combine the numerator into a single fraction. \[ 1+\dfrac{2}{x} = \dfrac{x+2}{x} \]
Step 2: Combine the denominator into a single fraction. \[ 1-\dfrac{4}{x^2} = \dfrac{x^2-4}{x^2} \]
Step 3: Dividing by a fraction means multiplying by its reciprocal — then factor and cancel. \[ \dfrac{x+2}{x} \div \dfrac{x^2-4}{x^2} = \dfrac{x+2}{x}\cdot\dfrac{x^2}{(x-2)(x+2)} = \dfrac{x}{x-2} \]

Method 2 — Fraction Busters: clear all the denominators at once

Step 1: Find the LCD of every denominator buried inside the expression — here that's \( x \) and \( x^2 \), so the LCD is \( x^2 \).
Step 2: Multiply the top and bottom of the whole complex fraction by \( x^2 \), then distribute through every term. \[ \dfrac{x^2\left(1+\dfrac{2}{x}\right)}{x^2\left(1-\dfrac{4}{x^2}\right)} = \dfrac{x^2+2x}{x^2-4} \]
Step 3: No more fractions inside fractions — factor and cancel like any ordinary algebraic fraction. \[ \dfrac{x^2+2x}{x^2-4} = \dfrac{x(x+2)}{(x-2)(x+2)} = \dfrac{x}{x-2} \]
Answer (either method): \( \dfrac{x}{x-2} \), with \( x \neq 0, 2, -2 \)
⚠ Common mistake: Whenever you clear denominators — Method 2 in either example above — multiply every single term by the LCD, including terms that don't have a visible fraction. In Example 2's Method 2, the "2" on the right side still gets multiplied by \( 4x \); in Example 3's Method 2, the "1" in both the numerator and denominator still gets multiplied by \( x^2 \). Skipping a term is the single most common error with this technique.
💡 Worth noticing: Both examples above show the same two philosophies — Method 1 combines everything into a single fraction first, then clears that one fraction; Method 2 clears every denominator immediately, before combining anything. This holds whether you're solving an equation (Example 2) or simplifying an expression (Example 3). Method 1 tends to feel more familiar since it mirrors adding fractions from earlier courses; Method 2 is often faster once you're comfortable with it. Neither is "more correct" — use whichever one you can execute reliably under pressure.

2. Check-It Exercises

Work each problem on paper first. Then reveal the full solution and compare your steps, not just your final answer.

Check-It 1

Simplify: \( \dfrac{x^2-9}{x^2-x-6} \)

\( x^2-9=(x-3)(x+3) \); \( x^2-x-6=(x-3)(x+2) \). Cancel \( (x-3) \): \( \dfrac{x+3}{x+2} \)

Check-It 2

Simplify: \( \dfrac{x^2+5x+6}{x^2-4} \)

\( x^2+5x+6=(x+2)(x+3) \); \( x^2-4=(x-2)(x+2) \). Cancel \( (x+2) \): \( \dfrac{x+3}{x-2} \)

Check-It 3

Solve using Fraction Busters: \( \dfrac{x}{3} - \dfrac{2}{x} = \dfrac{1}{3} \)

Multiply every term by \( 3x \): \( x^2 - 6 = x \implies x^2-x-6=0 \implies (x-3)(x+2)=0 \implies x=3 \text{ or } x=-2 \)

Check-It 4

Solve using Fraction Busters: \( \dfrac{2}{x} + \dfrac{1}{2} = \dfrac{3}{x} \)

Multiply every term by \( 2x \): \( 4 + x = 6 \implies x=2 \)

Check-It 5

Simplify: \( \dfrac{\dfrac{3}{x}+1}{\dfrac{9}{x^2}-1} \)

Numerator: \( \dfrac{3+x}{x} \). Denominator: \( \dfrac{9-x^2}{x^2} \). Divide and factor \( 9-x^2=(3-x)(3+x) \): \( \dfrac{3+x}{x}\cdot\dfrac{x^2}{(3-x)(3+x)} = \dfrac{x}{3-x} \)

Check-It 6

Simplify: \( \dfrac{x^3-27}{x^2-9} \)

Factor the numerator as a difference of cubes: \( x^3-27=(x-3)(x^2+3x+9) \). Factor the denominator: \( x^2-9=(x-3)(x+3) \). Cancel \( (x-3) \): \( \dfrac{x^2+3x+9}{x+3} \)
